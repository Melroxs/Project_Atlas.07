# DB-001 — Atlas Domain Model**Document ID:** DB-001**Version:** 1.0**Status:** Approved**Owner:** Atlas Engineering**Last Updated:** July 2026---# PurposeThis document defines the complete business domain model for Atlas.The Domain Model is the highest-level representation of the Atlas platform. It identifies every major business entity, the ownership of that entity, and how domains interact.Every future database table, API endpoint, TypeScript model, AI workflow, and user interface should trace back to one of these domains.---# Domain PrinciplesAtlas follows these principles:- Multi-tenant by design.- Every business record belongs to an Organization.- AI never owns business data.- Evidence always supports recommendations.- Human approval precedes high-impact actions.- Soft deletion is preferred over permanent deletion.- Auditability is built into every domain.---# Domain Overview```Atlas Platform├── Platform│├── Identity│├── CRM│├── Claims│├── Documents│├── Photos│├── Evidence│├── Artificial Intelligence│├── Revenue Customers- Properties- Documents- Photos- AI- Evidence- Supplements- Compliance- ReportingEvidence connects:- Documents- Photos- OCR- AI- SupplementsVoice connects:- AI- Claims- Tasks- Reporting- UsersNo domain should bypass the ownership and audit model established by the Platform and Identity domains.---# Aggregate RootsThe following entities are aggregate roots:- Organization- User- Customer- Claim- Document- Photo- Supplement- Workflow- ReportAll child entities should be accessed through these aggregates where appropriate.---# Implementation OrderThe recommended implementation sequence is:1. Platform2. Identity3. CRM4. Claims5. Documents6. Photos7. Workflow8. Evidence9. AI10. Compliance11. Revenue Recovery12. Reporting13. Voice14. Integrations15. AdministrationEach domain should be fully implemented before work begins on the next.---# Success CriteriaThe Domain Model is complete when:- Every business capability belongs to a defined domain.- Every entity has a clear owner.- Cross-domain relationships are documented.- Aggregate roots are identified.- The implementation order is agreed upon.- Future database design can proceed from this model.
