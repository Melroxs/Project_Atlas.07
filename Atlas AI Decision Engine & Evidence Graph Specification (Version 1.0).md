Atlas AI Decision Engine & Evidence Graph Specification (Version 1.0)1. PurposeThe AI Decision Engine is responsible for analyzing all available claim information, identifying supplement opportunities, validating recommendations against available evidence and compliance rules, and producing explainable, structured outputs for human review.Unlike a traditional AI chatbot, Atlas does not make decisions in isolation. Every recommendation must be traceable to supporting evidence and remain subject to human approval.2. Design PrinciplesThe AI Decision Engine must be:Explainable.Evidence-driven.Deterministic where possible.Human-supervised.Auditable.Modular.Provider-independent.No recommendation should be generated without referencing the evidence used to support it.3. InputsThe engine consumes information from multiple modules:Claim DataClaim information.Customer.Property.Carrier.Adjuster.Timeline.DocumentsOCR text.Metadata.Classifications.Estimates.Reports.Correspondence.PhotosDamage categories.AI observations.Annotations.Before/after comparisons.User InputNotes.Tasks.Manual observations.Overrides.AI InterviewGuided inspection answers.Missing information.Clarifications.Compliance RulesRequired evidence.Required documentation.Business policies.4. Evidence GraphThe Evidence Graph is the central intelligence layer.Instead of treating every document independently, Atlas builds relationships between all evidence.Claim │ ├── Documents │      │ │      ├── OCR │      ├── Metadata │      └── AI Summary │ ├── Photos │      │ │      ├── Damage Areas │      ├── AI Analysis │      └── Annotations │ ├── Interviews │ ├── Notes │ └── Timeline        │        ▼   Evidence Graph        │        ▼ Decision EngineThis graph allows Atlas to reason across multiple sources rather than relying on a single document or image.5. Decision PipelineEvery recommendation follows the same pipeline:Collect Evidence        ↓Normalize Data        ↓Build Evidence Graph        ↓Identify Opportunities        ↓Evaluate Business Rules        ↓Run Compliance Checks        ↓Calculate Confidence        ↓Generate Recommendation        ↓Human ReviewThis pipeline ensures consistency and transparency.6. Evidence NormalizationBefore analysis, Atlas standardizes incoming data by:Removing duplicate information.Resolving conflicting values.Standardizing dates.Standardizing measurements and units.Mapping entities (carrier, adjuster, property).Linking related documents and photos.The normalized data becomes the foundation for reasoning.7. Opportunity DetectionThe engine identifies potential supplement opportunities by comparing available evidence with the existing estimate.Examples include:Missing line items.Additional damaged materials.Equipment not included.Labor omissions.Code-required upgrades.Overlooked mitigation work.Supporting documentation not yet referenced.Detected opportunities are suggestions until reviewed by a human.8. Recommendation GenerationEach recommendation should include:Title.Description.Suggested action.Estimated impact.Business rationale.Supporting evidence.Confidence score.Compliance status.Recommendations should be concise, actionable, and traceable.9. Confidence ScoringEvery recommendation receives a confidence score based on factors such as:Quantity of supporting evidence.Quality of evidence.Agreement across documents and photos.AI certainty.Completeness of the claim record.Compliance readiness.Suggested confidence bands:90–100%: Strong evidence; ready for review.70–89%: Good evidence; minor verification recommended.50–69%: Partial evidence; additional documentation suggested.Below 50%: Insufficient evidence; do not recommend submission.Confidence informs review priority but does not replace human judgment.10. ExplainabilityEvery AI recommendation must answer:Why was this recommendation generated?Which evidence supports it?Which documents were referenced?Which photos were referenced?Which business rules applied?Which compliance checks were performed?What assumptions were made?Users should never see an unexplained recommendation.11. Human Review WorkflowAtlas is designed with a human-in-the-loop model.Reviewers should be able to:Accept recommendations.Modify recommendations.Reject recommendations.Request additional evidence.Add reviewer notes.Record override reasons where applicable.Every decision should be logged in the audit trail.12. Compliance IntegrationBefore a recommendation is eligible for submission, Atlas should verify:Required documents are present.Required photos are linked.Supporting evidence exists.Business rules are satisfied.Mandatory fields are complete.If validation fails, the engine should identify the missing requirements instead of simply rejecting the recommendation.13. Decision RecordEach AI decision should persist:Decision ID.Claim ID.Model version.Timestamp.Inputs used.Recommendation.Confidence score.Supporting evidence.Reviewer outcome.Final status.This creates a complete history for auditing and continuous improvement.14. Learning & FeedbackThe MVP will not automatically retrain models from customer data.Instead, Atlas should capture structured feedback such as:Recommendation accepted.Recommendation modified.Recommendation rejected.Reason for rejection.Additional evidence added.This feedback can be used to improve prompts, business rules, or future model evaluations while keeping customer control over their data.15. AI Provider AbstractionThe Decision Engine should not depend on a single AI provider.An abstraction layer should allow Atlas to support:OpenAI-compatible APIs.Self-hosted models.Future enterprise AI providers.Changing providers should not require changes to the business logic.16. Failure HandlingIf AI processing cannot complete:Preserve all claim data.Record the error.Notify the user if appropriate.Allow retry.Continue manual workflows without blocking claim progress.AI failures should never prevent users from managing claims.17. Security & PrivacyThe engine should:Process only authorized claim data.Respect organization boundaries.Protect sensitive information.Log AI requests and responses where appropriate.Avoid exposing confidential information in prompts beyond what is necessary.18. ReportingThe engine should provide metrics such as:Recommendations generated.Acceptance rate.Modification rate.Rejection rate.Average confidence score.AI processing time.Most common recommendation categories.Evidence completeness trends.These metrics help evaluate AI effectiveness without replacing operational reporting.19. Future EnhancementsThe architecture should support:Carrier-specific recommendation profiles.State-specific compliance rules.Predictive claim outcomes.Cost estimation assistance.Multi-modal reasoning across text, images, and future video inputs.Voice-assisted claim review.Continuous prompt evaluation and benchmarking.20. Acceptance CriteriaThe AI Decision Engine is complete when it can:Ingest structured claim data from all core modules.Build an evidence graph linking documents, photos, notes, and interviews.Identify supplement opportunities.Generate explainable recommendations.Calculate confidence scores.Validate recommendations against compliance rules.Support human review and overrides.Persist complete decision records for auditing.Continue operating safely even when AI services are unavailable.Documentation ProgressCompleted specifications:✅ System Architecture✅ Repository Structure & Development Environment✅ Database Architecture & Data Model✅ Authentication & Authorization✅ API Architecture & Service Layer✅ Frontend Architecture & Design System✅ Claims Management Module✅ Document Management & Intelligence Module✅ Photo Management & Visual Intelligence Module✅ AI Decision Engine & Evidence GraphNext Document: Supplement Generation & Revenue Recovery EngineThis specification will define Atlas's core business value: how evidence-backed supplement packages are assembled, how line items are generated and justified, how compliance is enforced, how packages are exported, and how revenue recovery is tracked.
